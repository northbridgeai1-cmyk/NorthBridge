# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A possible small market to own.** Bilingual, done-for-you automation, run from Quito and delivered in person there and remotely across LatAm, is a narrower and less crowded place to start than "AI automation for US SMBs". If one city and one vertical can be dominated, the lens has something to work with.
- **Client-owned n8n accounts and quarterly reviews** set up a relationship that builds switching costs: the agency ends up knowing each client's workflows and data, and that knowledge piles up over time.
- **Near-zero cash burn** ($20/month, $500 launch budget) means the business can survive long enough to look for its niche without outside money.
- **A concrete wedge.** Running the payment-chasing demo live on real invoices is one specific job, and the business can be known for it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** There are zero paying clients, and every conversation so far has come through friends and family. Nothing yet shows how the first 100 customers arrive or what each one costs, so as things stand this is a product without a channel.
2. **Pure competition with no defensible edge.** In 2026 many agencies and freelancers sell the same thing, and buyers can do it themselves in Zapier, Make or n8n. The workflows are built on an open tool in accounts the client owns, which is good for trust but means a copycat or a cheaper freelancer can take a client over by month six. The business has no proprietary technology, no network effect, no economies of scale and no brand yet.
3. **Too many markets at once.** Four practices, a stock-research app, two continents and the US-vs-LatAm decision still open mean it starts with a tiny share of several large markets, not a big share of one small market. The capacity cap (5 new clients a month, founder time as the real cost) limits it even within one market. PerceptFolio is a separate business in a separate crowded market, and it dilutes focus and brand.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single market (one city or country, one industry, one language) could NorthBridge become the obvious choice in within 12 months? How many businesses are in it? That number is unknown today.
- What do you know about back-office pain in Ecuadorian or LatAm SMBs that US agencies and DIY tools do not? Local invoicing, tax e-billing (SRI) or WhatsApp-first collections could be a secret worth building on.
- How do clients 1 to 20 arrive without friends and family, and what does each cost in hours or dollars? Neither figure exists yet.
- What gets better with every client you add, such as reusable workflow templates, benchmarks or referrals within one industry, so that client 50 is cheaper to serve and harder to copy than client 5?
- Why now? What has changed in 2026 that makes this the moment, beyond "AI is popular"?
- Will this team still be standing in a year? The partnership split, roles and hours are not agreed.
- Why keep PerceptFolio under the same roof?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one beachhead within 30 days:** one geography, one vertical and one lead offer (the payment-chasing pilot). Put the other practices and PerceptFolio on hold from the front of the sales effort.
2. **Prove a repeatable channel that does not run through friends and family:** at least 5 paying clients from strangers within 90 days, with cost and time per client recorded.
3. **Name and start building one durable advantage** in that niche, such as vertical-specific templates, local integrations that US competitors lack, or a referral loop within the industry, and show that it compounds by client 10.
4. **Sign a partnership agreement** (split, roles, hours) before the first paid engagement.
